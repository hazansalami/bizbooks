# BizBooks Insights: content plan

**Audience:** owners, finance leads and operations managers at registered Nigerian companies where invoicing
is native: digital and creative agencies, software/IT firms, consultancies, marketing and PR, engineering,
logistics and B2B services (typically 2–50 staff, ₦20m–₦2bn turnover).

**What they're searching for (2026):** the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 took effect on 1 January 2026 and changed PAYE
bands, company tax for small companies, VAT input recovery and the tax authority itself (FIRS → NRS). WHT rules
changed in 2025. E-invoicing is rolling out from 2026. Owners are confused, and search demand is high. That is
the opening.

**Approach (content-strategy + ai-seo skills):**
- Five pillars, each a hub with spokes. Hubs are built first.
- Every article opens with a 40–60 word direct answer, uses question-shaped H2s, includes tables and worked
  examples in naira, cites primary or authoritative sources, shows "last updated", and ends with a FAQ
  (FAQPage schema) and a sources list.
- Link-earning formats get priority: the tax deadline calendar (stat/date roundup), calculators (PAYE,
  VAT/WHT) and a glossary.
- The mix is 60% searchable, 30% shareable/opinion, 10% tools and experiments.
- Each piece ends with a soft, relevant product link. No hard sell inside explainers.

**Scoring:** Customer impact 40% · Content–market fit 30% · Search potential 20% · Resources 10% (1–10 each).

## Before publishing

- Have an ICAN or CITN-qualified reviewer check each tax and payroll article, then add their name and
  credentials to the "Reviewed by" line in the frontmatter (`reviewer:`). This is the biggest E-E-A-T lift available.
- Refresh tax pages every quarter. The `updated:` date shows on every article.
- Distribution: turn each hub into a LinkedIn article (front-load the target phrase), and do a Twitter/X thread
  for the tax calendar every January and July.
