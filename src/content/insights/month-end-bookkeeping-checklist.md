Most companies do their books at year end, in a panic, a week before the auditor arrives. The companies that always know their numbers do a **short close every month**. Here is the checklist. Print it, or work through it in BizBooks.

## The month-end checklist

### Money in

1. **Record every invoice issued this month**, including recurring invoices.
2. **Match every payment received** to its invoice. Check bank statements for transfers that weren't flagged.
3. **Review aged receivables.** Who owes you, and how late? Send reminders and escalate anything over 30 days. See [how to get clients to pay on time](/insights/how-to-get-clients-to-pay-on-time).
4. **Log WHT deducted by clients** and chase any missing credit notes.

### Money out

5. **Record every expense**, with a receipt or supplier invoice attached, and note any **VAT** on it (it's recoverable in 2026).
6. **Record unpaid supplier bills** with their due dates, so you know what you owe.
7. **Record payroll**: gross salaries, employer pension and statutory contributions.

### Bank and cash

8. **Reconcile each bank account.** The balance in your books should match the statement. Investigate every difference. Bank charges, stamp duty and transfer fees are the usual culprits.

### Tax

9. **Prepare this month's remittances**, due next month:
   - **PAYE** → State IRS by the **10th**
   - **Pension** → PFAs within **7 working days** of pay day
   - **VAT return** (output VAT − input VAT) → NRS by the **21st**
   - **WHT** deducted from suppliers → NRS by the **21st** (companies) or State IRS (individuals)

   See the full [2026 tax calendar](/insights/nigeria-tax-calendar-2026).

### Review

10. **Read your profit and loss.** Are income and expenses where you expected? Is any category unusually high? See [how to read a P&L](/insights/profit-and-loss-statement-explained).
11. **Check cash flow**: money in against money out, and what's due in the next 30 days (payroll, bills, tax).
12. **File everything**: receipts, supplier invoices, WHT credit notes and payslips, stored where your accountant can find them.

## How long should month-end take?

For a company with a few dozen transactions a month, **2–4 hours** is realistic if you record things as they happen. The biggest time sink is hunting for missing receipts and unmatched transfers. Fix that by snapping receipts the day you pay.

## What do I send my accountant?

At minimum: the **profit and loss**, **aged receivables and payables**, a **list of all transactions** (CSV), **VAT and payroll summaries**, and copies of receipts and WHT credit notes. If your books are clean monthly, year-end accounts and tax returns cost less and take less time.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a month-end close?

It's the routine of recording, checking and reviewing all of a month's transactions, so your books are accurate before you start the next month.

### Why reconcile bank accounts every month?

Reconciliation catches missing income, duplicate payments, bank charges and fraud early, while the details are still fresh.

### Do small companies need monthly bookkeeping?

Yes. Monthly VAT, PAYE and WHT deadlines depend on accurate monthly figures, and clean books make annual returns much cheaper.
