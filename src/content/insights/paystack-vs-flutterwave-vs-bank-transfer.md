Most Nigerian corporate clients pay by bank transfer, so why bother with a payment gateway? Because the fee is often smaller than you think, and the time saved matching payments is bigger than you think.

## How do the fees compare?

Local (naira) payment fees as published by the providers in 2026. Check their pricing pages for current rates:

| | Paystack | Flutterwave | Direct bank transfer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local fee | **1.5% + ₦100** (₦100 waived under ₦2,500) | **1.4%** | Usually free to receive |
| Cap per local transaction | **₦2,000** | **₦2,000** | – |
| VAT on fees | 7.5% on the fee | 7.5% on the fee | – |
| Payment methods | Card, bank transfer, USSD, and more | Card, bank transfer, USSD, and more | Transfer only |
| Confirmation | Instant, automatic | Instant, automatic | You check your bank app |
| Settlement | To your bank on the provider's schedule | To your bank on the provider's schedule | Immediate |

## What does the ₦2,000 cap mean for B2B?

On large invoices the cap makes gateway fees tiny as a percentage:

| Invoice | Paystack fee (capped) | As % of invoice |
|---|---|---|
| ₦50,000 | ₦850 | 1.7% |
| ₦500,000 | ₦2,000 | 0.4% |
| ₦3,000,000 | ₦2,000 | 0.07% |

(Before VAT on the fee.) For a ₦3m invoice, ₦2,000 buys instant confirmation and automatic reconciliation.

## When is bank transfer better?

- The client's finance team **only pays by transfer** from their ERP. Many large corporates do.
- **Very frequent, small internal transfers** where fees add up.
- You want funds **immediately** rather than on the gateway's settlement schedule.

The downside is **reconciliation**: matching a transfer to an invoice when the narration says "Payment" and the sender is a different entity from the client name.

## When is a payment link better?

- **SMEs and individuals** who'd rather pay by card or USSD in the moment
- **Deposits and mobilisation fees**, where speed wins the project
- **Retainers**, where a link in every invoice email gets paid faster
- Anywhere you want the invoice **marked paid automatically**

## Paystack or Flutterwave?

Both are reliable, widely used and CBN-licensed. Differences are mostly in the details: fees at different price points, dashboard preferences, settlement timing and which one your team already uses. If you have neither, pick one, compare their current pricing pages, and don't over-think it.

## The best setup: offer both

Put a **"Pay now" link** and your **bank details** on every invoice. Clients who can pay online will, and the payment is recorded automatically. Clients who must transfer can, and a **"we've sent it" button** tells you to check.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are Paystack's fees in Nigeria?

For local payments, 1.5% + ₦100 (with the ₦100 waived under ₦2,500), capped at ₦2,000 per transaction, plus VAT on the fee. Check Paystack's pricing page for current rates.

### What are Flutterwave's fees in Nigeria?

Flutterwave publishes a 1.4% fee on local naira payments, capped at ₦2,000 per transaction. Check its pricing page for current rates.

### Is a payment gateway worth it for large invoices?

Usually yes. Because local fees are capped at ₦2,000, the cost on a large invoice is a tiny percentage, and you get instant confirmation and automatic reconciliation.

### Do clients still pay by bank transfer?

Many Nigerian corporates do. Offer both a payment link and your bank details on every invoice.
