In June 2025 President Tinubu signed four tax reform laws: the **Nigeria Tax Act (NTA)**, the **Nigeria Tax Administration Act (NTAA)**, the **Nigeria Revenue Service (Establishment) Act** and the **Joint Revenue Board Act**. Most of their provisions took effect on **1 January 2026**. Together they replace a patchwork of older laws (the Companies Income Tax Act, Personal Income Tax Act, VAT Act and others) with one main tax law.

If you run a registered company, most of what you did before still applies: you still charge VAT, deduct PAYE and WHT, and file annual returns. What changed is *who pays what*, *what you can claim back* and *who you deal with*. This guide covers the changes that matter most for service companies such as agencies, consultancies and IT firms.

## What are the biggest changes for companies?

| Area | Before 2026 | From 1 January 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Tax authority | Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) | **Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS)** |
| Small company income tax | 0% for turnover under ₦25m | **0% for turnover up to ₦100m and fixed assets up to ₦250m** (professional services excluded) |
| Other levies on profit | Tertiary Education Tax, IT levy, NASENI levy, Police Trust Fund levy | **One 4% development levy** on assessable profit (small companies exempt) |
| Standard company income tax | 30% (medium companies 20%) | 30% for companies that aren't small |
| VAT rate | 7.5% | **7.5%** (unchanged) |
| Input VAT on services and assets | Largely not recoverable | **Recoverable** when it relates to your taxable supplies |
| PAYE | Consolidated Relief Allowance, top rate 24% | **First ₦800,000 tax-free**, bands from 15% to 25%, rent relief replaces CRA |

## Is my company now tax-free?

Possibly. Under the NTA, a **small company** is one with **annual gross turnover of ₦100 million or less** *and* **total fixed assets of ₦250 million or less**. Small companies pay **0% company income tax** and are exempt from capital gains tax and the development levy.

There's an important catch for service businesses: **companies that provide professional services cannot be small companies**, whatever their size. If you're a consultancy, law firm, accounting practice or engineering firm, plan to pay company income tax. Whether a digital agency counts as "professional services" depends on what it actually does, so ask your tax adviser.

A small company still has to **file its annual returns**. A 0% rate isn't the same as not filing. Read our full guide to the [small company tax exemption](/insights/small-company-tax-exemption-nigeria).

## What is the 4% development levy?

The development levy is a single **4% charge on assessable profits** for companies that aren't small. It replaces four separate levies: Tertiary Education Tax, the IT levy, the NASENI levy and the Police Trust Fund levy. It's charged on profit *before* capital allowances and losses are deducted, so it can be higher than people expect.

**Example:** A consultancy with ₦180m assessable profit pays ₦7.2m development levy (4% × ₦180m), on top of company income tax. Estimate yours with the [company income tax calculator](/tools/company-income-tax-calculator).

## What changed about VAT?

The rate stays at **7.5%**, but two things matter for service companies:

1. **You can now claim input VAT on services and fixed assets.** VAT you pay on software, professional fees, equipment and similar costs can be offset against the VAT you charge, as long as it relates to your taxable supplies.
2. **Small companies are exempt from charging VAT.** The same small-company definition applies, and professional services are excluded. Many corporate clients still expect VAT invoices, so check before switching VAT off.

VAT returns are still due by the **21st of the following month**. See our [VAT guide for service companies](/insights/vat-in-nigeria-for-service-companies).

## What changed for payroll?

PAYE now uses these annual bands:

| Annual taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| First ₦800,000 | 0% |
| Next ₦2,200,000 (₦800,001–₦3m) | 15% |
| Next ₦9,000,000 (₦3m–₦12m) | 18% |
| Next ₦13,000,000 (₦12m–₦25m) | 21% |
| Next ₦25,000,000 (₦25m–₦50m) | 23% |
| Above ₦50,000,000 | 25% |

The Consolidated Relief Allowance is gone. In its place, employees can claim **rent relief of 20% of annual rent, up to ₦500,000**. Pension (8%), NHF and some other contributions remain deductible. Work through the numbers with our [PAYE guide and calculator](/insights/how-to-calculate-paye-in-nigeria).

## What changed about withholding tax?

WHT is governed by the **Deduction of Tax at Source (Withholding) Regulations 2024**, which apply from 2025 and continue under the new regime. Resident rates are mostly **5%** for consultancy, professional, technical and management fees and commissions, and **2%** for supply of goods and construction contracts. Small companies don't have to deduct WHT when the supplier has a valid TIN and the transaction is ₦2 million or less in the month. See [withholding tax in Nigeria 2026](/insights/withholding-tax-in-nigeria).

## Who collects taxes now?

The **Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS)** replaces the FIRS and collects federal taxes: company income tax, VAT, the development levy and WHT on payments to companies. **PAYE** and WHT on payments to individuals still go to the **State Internal Revenue Service** of the state where the employee or individual lives.

## What should a company do now?

1. **Check whether you're a small company** using last year's turnover and your fixed asset register, and whether your work counts as professional services.
2. **Start keeping supplier VAT invoices** so you can claim input VAT.
3. **Update payroll to the 2026 PAYE bands** and collect rent evidence from staff who want rent relief.
4. **Review your WHT deductions** against the 2024 rates, and ask clients for credit notes for WHT they deduct from you.
5. **Put every deadline in one calendar**: VAT and WHT by the 21st, PAYE by the 10th. Here is our [2026 tax calendar](/insights/nigeria-tax-calendar-2026).

## Frequently asked questions

### When did the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 take effect?

Most provisions of the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 took effect on 1 January 2026. The four reform laws were signed on 26 June 2025.

### Has the VAT rate increased in 2026?

No. VAT remains 7.5%. What changed is that businesses can now recover input VAT on services and fixed assets that relate to taxable supplies, and small companies are exempt from charging VAT.

### Do small companies still need to file tax returns?

Yes. A small company pays 0% company income tax but must still register, file annual returns and keep proper records.

### Is FIRS still the tax authority?

No. The Federal Inland Revenue Service has been replaced by the Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS). State Internal Revenue Services still collect PAYE.

### Does the new law change how much PAYE my staff pay?

Yes. The first ₦800,000 of annual taxable income is now tax-free, and the rates run from 15% to 25%. Most lower and middle earners pay less than before. Some high earners pay more.
