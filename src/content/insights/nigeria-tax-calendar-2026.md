Missing a tax deadline in Nigeria costs money: penalties plus interest, and sometimes a tax clearance certificate you can't get when a big contract needs it. Here is every recurring deadline a typical Nigerian company faces in 2026, in one place. Bookmark it, or let BizBooks track it for you.

## What are the monthly tax deadlines in Nigeria?

| Deadline | What | Paid to | Applies if you… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10th** of next month | **PAYE** deducted from staff salaries | State Internal Revenue Service (where each employee lives) | have employees |
| **Within 7 working days** of paying salaries | **Pension** (8% employee + 10% employer) | Each employee's Pension Fund Administrator | have 3 or more employees |
| **21st** of next month | **VAT** return and payment | Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) | are VAT registered |
| **21st** of next month | **WHT** deducted from payments to companies | NRS | deduct WHT from suppliers |
| As set by the state | **WHT** deducted from payments to individuals | State Internal Revenue Service | pay individual contractors |
| Monthly (with payroll) | **NHF** (2.5% of basic) for registered staff | Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria | have NHF-registered staff |
| Monthly | **NSITF** (1% of payroll) | NSITF | have employees |

## What are the annual deadlines?

| Deadline | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **31 January** | **Employer's annual PAYE return (Form H1)** for the previous year | Summarises each employee's pay and tax for the year. Filed with the State IRS. |
| **Within 6 months of year end** (30 June for a December year end) | **Company income tax return** with audited accounts | Required even for small companies paying 0%. |
| **Within 6 months of year end** | **Development levy** (4% of assessable profit) | Not payable by small companies. |
| Annually | **ITF contribution** (1% of annual payroll) | For employers with 5+ staff or ₦50m+ turnover. |
| Annually (by the CAC deadline) | **CAC annual returns** | A corporate filing, not a tax, but needed to stay in good standing. |

## What does a typical month look like?

For a company with a December year end, paying salaries on the 25th:

| Date | Task |
|---|---|
| 25 September | Run payroll and pay salaries |
| By 6 October (7 working days) | Remit September pension to PFAs |
| 10 October | Remit September PAYE to State IRS |
| 21 October | File and pay September VAT; remit September WHT |
| End of October | Close October's books |

## How much should I set aside?

Keep tax money separate from operating cash, ideally in a **dedicated account**. A simple rule for VAT-registered service companies:

- Move **7.5% of every client payment** that includes VAT into the tax account. That's your output VAT.
- When you run payroll, move **PAYE + pension** at the same time you pay net salaries.
- Every quarter, set aside an estimate of **company income tax and development levy** based on your profit to date.

## What if I miss a deadline?

Penalties and interest apply for late filing and late payment under the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025, and repeated defaults can hold up your **tax clearance certificate**. If you've missed one, **file and pay as soon as you can**, since interest keeps growing, and talk to your tax adviser about the exact penalty for your case.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is PAYE due in Nigeria?

PAYE deducted from salaries must be remitted to the State Internal Revenue Service by the 10th of the following month.

### When is VAT due in Nigeria?

VAT returns and payments are due by the 21st of the month after the month of supply.

### When must pension contributions be remitted?

Employers must remit employee and employer pension contributions to each employee's Pension Fund Administrator within 7 working days of paying salaries.

### When is the company income tax return due?

Within six months after the end of the company's financial year. For a December year end, that's 30 June of the following year.

### When is the annual PAYE return (Form H1) due?

By 31 January for the previous year, filed with the State Internal Revenue Service.
