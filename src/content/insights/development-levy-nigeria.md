Before 2026, a profitable Nigerian company could face four separate levies on top of company income tax. The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 merged them into one: the **development levy**. It's simpler to understand, but it's charged on a wider base than many owners expect.

## What is the development levy?

The development levy is a **4% levy on assessable profit** payable by companies that are **not small companies**. It replaces:

| Old levy | Old rate (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Tertiary Education Tax (TETFund) | 3% of assessable profit |
| National Information Technology Development (NITDA) levy | 1% of profit before tax (larger companies) |
| NASENI levy | 0.25% of profit before tax (certain sectors) |
| Police Trust Fund levy | 0.005% of net profit |

## Who has to pay it?

Every company that doesn't qualify as a **small company** under the NTA. A small company has turnover of ₦100m or less, fixed assets of ₦250m or less, and provides no professional services. Small companies are exempt. Check yours with the [company income tax calculator](/tools/company-income-tax-calculator).

## How is it calculated?

**Development levy = 4% × assessable profit.**

Assessable profit is profit adjusted for tax purposes **before** deducting capital allowances and tax losses brought forward. That's the important detail: company income tax is calculated after those deductions, but the levy isn't. So a company with large capital allowances or past losses can owe the levy even when its company income tax bill is small.

## Worked example

A software company (not small) reports:

| | ₦ |
|---|---|
| Assessable profit | 60,000,000 |
| Less capital allowances | −15,000,000 |
| Less losses brought forward | −10,000,000 |
| **Taxable profit** | **35,000,000** |
| Company income tax (30% × ₦35m) | 10,500,000 |
| **Development levy (4% × ₦60m)** | **2,400,000** |
| **Total** | **12,900,000** |

The levy is 4% of **₦60m**, not ₦35m.

## When is it paid?

With your annual company income tax return: **within six months after your financial year end** (30 June for a December year end), to the **Nigeria Revenue Service**. Late payment attracts a 10% penalty plus interest under the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025.

## How should I plan for it?

- Estimate it quarterly alongside company income tax, and set cash aside monthly.
- Don't assume losses or capital allowances wipe out your tax bill. They reduce CIT, not the levy.
- If you're close to the small-company thresholds, track trailing 12-month turnover. Crossing ₦100m brings in both CIT and the levy.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the development levy rate in Nigeria?

4% of assessable profit for companies that are not small companies, from 2026 under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025.

### Do small companies pay the development levy?

No. Small companies (turnover up to ₦100m, fixed assets up to ₦250m, no professional services) are exempt.

### Is Tertiary Education Tax still payable?

No. Tertiary Education Tax and three other levies were replaced by the single development levy from 2026.

### Is the development levy calculated on taxable profit?

No. It's calculated on assessable profit, before capital allowances and losses brought forward, so it can be higher than people expect.
