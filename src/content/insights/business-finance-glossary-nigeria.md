Finance jargon makes simple things sound hard. Here are the terms you'll meet running a Nigerian company, each explained in a sentence or two.

## Accounting and reports

**Accrual accounting**: Recording income when you invoice and expenses when you incur them, whether or not cash has moved. It shows true performance. Compare *cash basis*.

**Aged payables**: Your unpaid bills grouped by how overdue they are (coming due, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days).

**Aged receivables**: Unpaid client invoices grouped by how overdue they are. The 90+ days column is where bad debts start.

**Balance sheet**: A snapshot of what the company owns (assets), owes (liabilities) and the owners' stake (equity) on a given date.

**Bill**: An invoice you've received from a supplier and haven't paid yet.

**Cash basis**: Recording income and expenses only when money actually moves. Simple, but it can hide what you owe and are owed.

**Cash flow**: Money coming in and going out over a period. A profitable company can still run short of cash.

**Chart of accounts**: The list of categories (income, expense, asset, liability accounts) your books are organised into.

**Cost of sales**: Direct costs of delivering your work, such as freelancers, production and media. Subtract them from income to get gross profit.

**Depreciation**: Spreading the cost of equipment over its useful life, instead of treating it all as an expense on day one.

**Gross profit**: Income minus cost of sales.

**Net profit**: What's left after all expenses. Before tax, unless stated.

**Profit and loss statement (P&L)**: Income minus expenses over a period. Also called the income statement. See [P&L explained](/insights/profit-and-loss-statement-explained).

**Reconciliation**: Checking that your books match your bank statement and explaining any differences.

**Retainer**: A fixed monthly fee for ongoing services, usually billed in advance.

## Tax

**CIT (company income tax)**: Tax on a company's profit: 30% for companies that aren't small, 0% for small companies from 2026.

**Development levy**: A 4% levy on assessable profit for companies that aren't small, replacing four older levies from 2026.

**E-invoicing (MBS)**: The NRS's Merchant-Buyer Solution, a mandatory electronic invoicing system being phased in from large to smaller taxpayers.

**Input VAT**: VAT you pay on business purchases. Since 2026 it's recoverable on services and fixed assets that relate to your taxable supplies.

**NRS (Nigeria Revenue Service)**: The federal tax authority that replaced the FIRS. It collects CIT, VAT, the development levy and WHT on payments to companies.

**NTA (Nigeria Tax Act 2025)**: The main tax law in force from 1 January 2026. See [the NTA explained](/insights/nigeria-tax-act-2025-explained-for-companies).

**Output VAT**: VAT you charge clients on your invoices.

**Small company**: Under the NTA, a company with turnover of ₦100m or less and fixed assets of ₦250m or less that doesn't provide professional services. It pays 0% CIT. See [the small company exemption](/insights/small-company-tax-exemption-nigeria).

**SIRS (State Internal Revenue Service)**: The state tax authority. It collects PAYE and WHT on payments to individuals.

**Tax clearance certificate (TCC)**: Proof that a company is up to date with its taxes, often needed for government and large corporate contracts.

**TIN (Tax Identification Number)**: Your company's tax ID. It belongs on every invoice.

**VAT (value added tax)**: A 7.5% tax on most goods and services, filed monthly by the 21st. See [VAT for service companies](/insights/vat-in-nigeria-for-service-companies).

**WHT (withholding tax)**: An advance tax deducted from payments, usually 5% on professional fees and 2% on supplies, and credited against the recipient's income tax. See [withholding tax](/insights/withholding-tax-in-nigeria).

**WHT credit note**: Proof that WHT deducted from you was remitted. You need it to claim the credit.

## Payroll

**Gross pay**: Total pay before deductions: basic, housing, transport and other allowances.

**ITF**: Industrial Training Fund: 1% of annual payroll for employers with 5+ staff or ₦50m+ turnover.

**Net pay (take-home)**: Gross pay minus PAYE, pension and other deductions.

**NHF**: National Housing Fund: 2.5% of basic salary for registered employees.

**NSITF**: Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund: 1% of monthly payroll paid by employers for the Employee Compensation Scheme.

**PAYE (pay as you earn)**: Income tax deducted from employees' salaries and remitted to the state by the 10th of the next month. See [how to calculate PAYE](/insights/how-to-calculate-paye-in-nigeria).

**Pension (contributory)**: 8% of pay from the employee and 10% from the employer, paid into the employee's retirement savings account.

**PFA (pension fund administrator)**: The licensed company that manages an employee's pension savings.

**Rent relief**: From 2026, 20% of an employee's annual rent (up to ₦500,000) is deducted before PAYE is calculated.

**RSA PIN**: The unique number for an employee's Retirement Savings Account.

## Getting paid

**Deposit / mobilisation fee**: A payment made before work starts, often 30–70% on projects. See [mobilisation fees and deposits](/insights/mobilisation-fees-and-deposits).

**Milestone billing**: Invoicing parts of a project when agreed deliverables are approved.

**PO (purchase order) number**: The client's internal reference for approving your work. Many companies won't pay an invoice without it.

**Payment terms**: How long a client has to pay, such as "net 30" (30 days from the invoice date).
