It's one of the most common questions owners ask accountants: "The report says we made ₦8m, so where is it?" The money is real. It's just not in your account yet, or it was never yours to begin with.

## 1. Clients haven't paid yet

Profit (on an accrual basis) counts income when you **invoice**. If clients pay 60 days later, that profit is sitting in **receivables**, not in your bank.

**Check:** your [aged receivables](/insights/aged-receivables-report). **Fix:** deposits, advance billing, reminders.

## 2. Some of the cash isn't yours

The VAT you collect, and the PAYE and pension you deduct from salaries, sit in your account until you remit them. That cash **looks** available but belongs to the government and your staff.

**Fix:** move tax money to a separate account as you receive or deduct it.

## 3. You bought equipment

A ₦6m laptop refresh leaves your account today, but in your profit and loss it's spread over several years as depreciation. Cash drops a lot, profit barely moves.

**Fix:** plan big purchases in your [cash flow forecast](/insights/cash-flow-forecast-service-business).

## 4. You're repaying loans

Loan **repayments** of principal reduce cash but aren't an expense. Only the **interest** hits profit.

## 5. You paid in advance

Annual software licences, rent paid a year upfront or insurance premiums reduce cash now, while the expense is spread across the months they cover.

## 6. Owners took money out

Dividends and director drawings reduce cash but aren't business expenses, so profit doesn't reflect them.

## 7. Timing

Month-end salaries, a VAT payment on the 21st, a large client payment arriving on the 2nd of next month: a snapshot on any single day can mislead.

## How do I see the whole picture?

Read three things together every month:

| Report | Tells you |
|---|---|
| Profit and loss (accrual) | Whether you're earning more than you spend |
| Cash flow | Where cash actually went |
| Aged receivables and payables | What's owed to you and what you owe |

## A quick reconciliation

**Profit for the period**
− increase in receivables (invoiced but not paid)
+ increase in unpaid bills and tax liabilities
− equipment purchases, loan principal, prepayments, owner drawings
= **change in cash** (roughly)

Your accountant does this formally as a cash flow statement. The rough version is enough to explain most gaps.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why doesn't my profit match my bank balance?

Because profit records income and costs when they're earned or incurred, while your bank records when money moves. Late-paying clients, taxes held, equipment, loans and owner drawings all create gaps.

### Can a company be profitable and still run out of cash?

Yes. Most often because clients pay late while salaries, rent and taxes are due on fixed dates.

### Is VAT part of my profit?

No. VAT you collect is owed to the government, so it's excluded from income and should be set aside.
